# Smart picture frame

A Wi-Fi digital picture frame with clock and music player built on the
**complete assembled Freenove FNK0086 Development Kit for ESP32-S3**.

* [firmware/](firmware/README.md) – the frame firmware (Wi-Fi setup on the touch
  screen, clock, slideshow from the microSD card, phone web page for uploading
  pictures and music, touch menu). Build and flash with PlatformIO.
* The rest of this folder is the printable enclosure, described below.

## Enclosure

A printable enclosure for the assembled kit. Nothing needs to be disconnected
for a compact display-only conversion.

**Status: dimensioned prototype, not physically fit-tested.** The manufacturer
publishes the assembled envelope, but not a complete mechanical drawing. A real
STL is not the same as a verified physical fit. Check the alignment template and
rear supports before printing/assembling the full enclosure.

## Open the model

Open [index.html](index.html) in a browser. It contains the actual printable mesh,
not a decorative approximation. Orbit, zoom, change color, hide parts, inspect
the rear, and explode the assembly. All scripts and mesh data are embedded; no
internet connection or server is needed.

### Fusion 360 version

[fusion/smart-picture-frame.f3d](fusion/smart-picture-frame.f3d) is the same design
as a native, parametric Fusion 360 assembly (File > Open on the .f3d). It was
generated by [fusion_design.py](fusion_design.py) through the FusionBridge add-in
and contains:

- **Picture frame case** (tilted 10 degrees, seated in the stand) with the
  components **Front shell**, **Rear cover** and a semi-transparent
  **Kit envelope (reference)** block (85 x 112 x 16 mm).
- **Desk stand** and **Alignment gauge** (lying flat beside the stand).
- 20 user parameters (Modify > Change Parameters): kit size, wall, bands,
  window size and the measured screen centre, stand angle, and the derived
  case size. The main extrude depths reference these parameters; sketch
  coordinates are numeric (regenerate from `dimensions.json` for other changes).
- A full timeline (44 features), each sketch/extrude named after what it does.

The run checked every body against the Manifold model (volumes agree within
0.01 cm3) and reported zero interference shell/cover, shell/kit, cover/kit and
case/stand; the case rests on the slot floor and the kit sits 1.0 mm above the
cover pads (foam-tape space). A STEP export is next to the .f3d. To rebuild:

```powershell
py -3.14 "C:\Users\dshohat\source\repos\3D Design\.github\skills\fusion-360-automation\scripts\fusion_client.py" run fusion_design.py --timeout 600
```

### Coordinate convention (why the STLs are reflected)

`build_model.py` works in front-view drawing coordinates: X to the viewer's right,
Y up, Z *into* the case. That triple is left-handed, so the case parts are reflected
in X when the STLs are written; the Fusion script instead models the case with Z
toward the viewer. Either way the physical part has the window on the viewer's
right, matching the kit. Earlier STLs (before this note) were mirror images.

The landscape and time on the display are an illustrative UI mockup, **not**
picture-frame firmware. The green kit block is an envelope reference, not a
detailed model of the electronics. The camera and heart-rate sensor are covered
in this design; it is intended for pictures/time, not full access to every demo.

## Print the check first

1. Open [alignment-template.svg](alignment-template.svg), print at **100% actual
   size**, and verify its calibration line measures exactly **50 mm**. Disable
   "fit to page". The sheet is a 1:1 dimensioned drawing of the assumed layout:
   kit outline, red frame window and camera circle, measured active screen, and
   dashed boxes for the speakers, boards and ports with their mm positions. Cut
   out the red window and the circle.
2. Place the template over the unpowered kit, with TOP at the speaker end. The
   85 x 112 mm outline should match the main kit footprint. Confirm that the
   entire active screen fits inside the window and the camera lens sits centred
   in the circle (the camera only rests on the WROOM module, so nudge it to fit;
   the FPC has enough slack for a couple of mm). This checks both screen position
   and envelope orientation. If the dashed boxes land on the wrong parts, the
   whole layout assumption is off; tell me the mm differences.
3. Alternatively print [alignment-gauge.stl](print/alignment-gauge.stl): an
   85 x 112 x 0.8 mm physical template with the same window and camera hole.
   Do not press it against the glass or exposed components.
4. If it does not align, adjust `screen_center_from_kit_left`,
   `screen_center_from_kit_bottom`, `camera_center_from_kit_left`,
   `camera_center_from_kit_bottom`, and/or the window dimensions in
   [dimensions.json](dimensions.json), regenerate, and repeat the check.
   Do not scale the STL to compensate; that also changes screw clearances.

The frame is sized from the kit, so the window sits where the screen really is:
right of centre and low. In portrait orientation the kit sits at X=3.4, Y=16.4 mm
within the frame; screen centre is X=61.9, Y=60.2 mm and the camera hole is at
X=16.9, Y=62.4 mm. X is right, Y is up from the frame bottom, Z runs from the
front face toward the back.

| [alignment-gauge.stl](print/alignment-gauge.stl) | 85 x 112 x 0.8 | Flat on bed |

All STLs are in **millimetres**, at 1:1 scale, with minimum Z=0. The rear cover
is flipped for printing; its seats protrude inward when assembled. The assembled
frame is 27.5 mm thick, not the sum of the separate part bounding-box depths.

Suggested starting settings: PLA or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 4 walls,
5 top/bottom layers, 15-20% infill. Every part fits a 150 x 150 mm bed (the
shell needs 92 x 140 mm plus brim). Small aperture/tongue/counterbore bridges
are at most about 2 mm. The stand slot is open at the top. Inspect the slicer
preview before printing; supports should generally not be necessary in the
supplied orientation. The front face will inherit the build-plate texture. No
sanding near electronics.

## Assembly and mounting

Required nonprinted items:

- Four **M3 x 10 mm thread-forming screws suitable for plastic**, nominal 6 mm
  head diameter or smaller. Printed pilot holes are 2.7 mm; confirm compatibility
  with your screws on a test piece. These are not heat-set-insert holes.
- Four approximately **6 x 6 x 1 mm removable, electrically insulating
  double-sided foam pads** for the rear corner seats.
- Optional non-slip feet for the desk plinth.

1. Disconnect power. Inspect the kit underside and identify four safe rear
   corner support areas. Pads must touch existing standoffs or component-free,
   mechanically sound areas, **never solder joints, pins, the antenna, or a
   battery**. The seat locations are inferred; change them in the generator if
   the real board differs. Do not force a mismatch.
2. Place foam pads on the rear-cover seats. Attach the kit by its safe rear
   support areas, front facing the front shell. The side walls leave 1 mm of
   clearance around the kit and locate it; nothing clips onto the board.
3. Connect USB **before** closing the enclosure. The two USB-C sockets point out
   of the kit's bottom edge; the plug sits in the 14 mm bottom band and the
   cable leaves through the 30 x 12 mm opening in the back of the cover, then
   through the matching notch in the plinth. A straight plug up to about 12 mm
   wide and 7 mm thick fits; a right-angle plug also works. Never force a bend.
4. Trial-fit the shell. The intended screen recess from the inside front
   surface is 1.2 mm (4.2 mm from the outside front). There must be **no pressure
   on the glass, camera, speaker cones, connectors, or wiring**. The kit is held
   by the padded rear seats, not squeezed between the front and back.
5. Seat the locating tongue and gently tighten four rear screws (two in the top
   band, two in the bottom band; the bottom-left one is shifted right to clear
   the USB bay). The shallow head recesses leave 1.8 mm of cover beneath the
   heads. Do not overtighten. The camera stays installed but is intentionally
   hidden. Use USB power; the design has no DC-jack cutout. The power switch is
   inside the case: switch on before closing, or leave the cover off until done.
6. Put the frame into the separate plinth, with the slot leaning away from you.
   Check stability on your actual surface and add non-slip feet. It is a desk
   enclosure, not a wall mount or drop-resistant product.
7. Power on and check ventilation during extended use. Keep rear vents clear.
   Stop if the enclosure gets unexpectedly hot. No internal battery is modeled.

The enclosure retains the entire assembled kit, but it cannot promise to fit
arbitrary jumper loops, existing rear stands, replacement camera modules, or
oversized USB plugs beyond the manufacturer's published envelope.

## Edit and regenerate

The model uses the Manifold solid kernel: actual boolean solids with STL
validation, not a stack of overlapping unjoined shapes.

```powershell
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python build_model.py
```

Edit [dimensions.json](dimensions.json) for sizes and clearances; the outer case
size is derived from the kit size plus walls, clearances and the two bands, so
you never set it directly. The `assumed_layout` list only drives the drawing and
the photo overlay, except for the screen fields that position the window.
[build_model.py](build_model.py) generates the parts, paper template, photo
overlay, validation report, model data, and self-contained preview from
[preview-template.html](preview-template.html). Do not edit the generated HTML
to change geometry.

[validation.json](validation.json) records STL round-trip watertightness,
orientation, connected-body count, volume, bounds, shell/back interference, and
the actual tilted enclosure/stand interference.
These tests verify CAD integrity, **not physical fit, print quality, thermal
performance, or desk stability**.
